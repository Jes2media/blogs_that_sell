ChatGPT wrote your landing page in 47 seconds.

You thought you'd save hours. Instead, you published copy that actively pushes buyers away—and you can't figure out why conversions tanked.

The problem isn't that AI wrote it. The problem is that AI makes specific, predictable mistakes that kill conversions. Once you know what to look for, you can fix them.

Here are the 11 mistakes ChatGPT makes—and exactly how to fix each one.

---

## Mistake #1: The Throat-Clearing Opening

ChatGPT loves to warm up before getting to the point:

> "In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, businesses of all sizes are discovering the transformative power of strategic content marketing. As more and more companies compete for attention online, the ability to create compelling copy has become more important than ever."

Two paragraphs. Zero information. The reader has already left.

**Why it kills conversions:** You have 3-5 seconds to earn attention. Throat-clearing wastes that window on generic statements that could introduce any product in any industry.

**The fix:** Delete everything before the first specific claim or concrete detail. Start where the actual content begins.

> "Your landing page converts at 1.2%. The industry benchmark is 5.8%. That gap is costing you $47,000 a month in lost revenue."

Same page, completely different energy. The reader knows immediately whether this is for them.

---

## Mistake #2: Benefit Stacking Without Evidence

ChatGPT lists benefits like a features catalog:

> "With our comprehensive solution, you'll:
> - Save valuable time and resources
> - Increase efficiency and productivity
> - Achieve better results faster
> - Scale your business with confidence
> - Unlock your full potential"

Sounds good. Means nothing. Every software company claims these exact same things.

**Why it kills conversions:** Unsupported claims trigger skepticism. Readers have seen these promises broken too many times. Generic benefits get mentally filed under "marketing BS."

**The fix:** Each benefit needs evidence—a number, a timeframe, a mechanism, or a story.

| Generic (ChatGPT) | Specific (Converts) |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| "Save valuable time" | "Save 6 hours per week on report generation" |
| "Increase efficiency" | "Cut your email response time from 4 hours to 20 minutes" |
| "Achieve better results" | "Our users see 34% higher conversion rates in the first 30 days" |

If you can't back a benefit with evidence, cut it. Three proven benefits beat seven vague ones.

## Mistake #3: The "Something for Everyone" Positioning

ChatGPT hedges to avoid excluding anyone:

> "Whether you're a small business owner, marketing professional, freelancer, or enterprise team leader, our platform is designed to meet your unique needs and help you achieve your goals."

Translation: this is for nobody in particular.

**Why it kills conversions:** When you speak to everyone, you resonate with no one. Readers need to see themselves specifically—not themselves plus five other demographics.

**The fix:** Pick your best-fit customer and write only to them. Let others self-select.

> "Built for B2B SaaS companies doing $1-10M ARR who've outgrown spreadsheets but aren't ready for enterprise pricing. If that's you, keep reading."

Some people will bounce. Good. The ones who stay are your actual buyers.

---

## Mistake #4: Passive Voice Everywhere

ChatGPT defaults to passive constructions:

> "Results are achieved through our innovative methodology. Your data is processed securely. Significant improvements can be expected within the first month."

Passive voice creates distance. It removes agency. It makes everything feel bureaucratic and impersonal.

**Why it kills conversions:** Passive voice weakens claims and bores readers. Active voice creates energy and assigns clear responsibility.

**The fix:** Find every instance of "is/are/was/were + past participle" and rewrite with a clear subject doing the action.

| Passive (Weak) | Active (Strong) |
|----------------|-----------------|
| "Results are achieved through..." | "You'll achieve results through..." |
| "Your data is processed securely" | "We process your data with bank-level encryption" |
| "Improvements can be expected" | "You'll see improvements within 30 days" |

Active voice is more direct, more confident, and more persuasive.

## Mistake #5: Corporate Jargon Camouflage

When ChatGPT doesn't know what to say, it hides behind jargon:

> "Our solution leverages cutting-edge technology to deliver synergistic outcomes that drive holistic transformation across your organization's key performance indicators."

This sentence communicates nothing. It's complexity masking emptiness.

**Why it kills conversions:** Jargon signals that you either don't understand your own product or you're trying to sound impressive rather than be useful. Neither builds trust.

**The fix:** Explain it like you're talking to a smart friend who doesn't work in your industry.

> "We help you send better emails. Our AI writes first drafts, tests subject lines, and tells you exactly when to hit send for maximum opens."

Simple. Clear. Actually says something.

---

## Mistake #6: The Missing "So What?"

ChatGPT describes features without connecting them to outcomes:

> "The platform includes automated workflows, real-time analytics, and seamless integrations with popular tools."

Okay. Why should I care?

**Why it kills conversions:** Features are meaningless until the reader understands how those features improve their life. The gap between feature and benefit is where sales are lost.

**The fix:** For every feature, complete this sentence: "...which means you can..."

> "The platform includes automated workflows **(which means you stop doing repetitive tasks manually)**, real-time analytics **(which means you see what's working before your next meeting, not next month)**, and seamless integrations **(which means you don't have to copy-paste data between six different tools)**."

Same features. Now they matter.

## Mistake #7: Urgency Without Reason

ChatGPT creates fake urgency that readers see through instantly:

> "Don't wait! This exclusive opportunity won't last forever. Act now to secure your spot before it's too late!"

Late for what? Why won't it last? What makes it exclusive?

**Why it kills conversions:** Fake urgency destroys trust. Readers have been burned by countdown timers that reset and "limited spots" that never fill. They're immune to manufactured pressure.

**The fix:** Use real urgency or none at all.

**Real urgency examples:**
- "Cohort starts March 15—we cap enrollment at 30 so everyone gets feedback."
- "This pricing reflects our beta launch. It goes up when we hit 500 users (we're at 412)."
- "I'm taking 3 new clients this quarter. Two spots are spoken for."

If you don't have real urgency, focus on the cost of inaction instead:

> "Every week you wait, your competitors are getting better at this. The gap doesn't close itself."

## Mistake #8: The Wall of Text Problem

ChatGPT writes in dense paragraphs with no visual hierarchy:

> "Our comprehensive approach to digital marketing encompasses a wide range of strategies and tactics designed to help businesses achieve their growth objectives. By combining data-driven insights with creative excellence, we deliver campaigns that resonate with target audiences and drive meaningful results. Our team of experienced professionals brings decades of combined expertise to every project, ensuring that each client receives personalized attention and customized solutions tailored to their specific needs and goals."

That's one paragraph. It should be five bullet points.

**Why it kills conversions:** People scan before they read. Walls of text get skipped entirely. If your key points are buried in dense paragraphs, they don't exist.

**The fix:** Break every paragraph at 2-3 sentences max. Use headers, bullets, and bold text to create entry points for scanners.

**The structure that converts:**
- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences)
- Subheaders every 200-300 words
- Bullets for lists of 3+ items
- Bold text on key phrases
- White space between sections

Make it scannable first. The readers who want depth will read closely once you've earned their attention.

## Mistake #9: Testimonials That Sound Fake

ChatGPT invents generic testimonials:

> "This product exceeded all my expectations. I highly recommend it to anyone looking for a comprehensive solution. The customer service is exceptional and the results speak for themselves." — Happy Customer

Nobody talks like this. It reads like marketing copy because it is marketing copy.

**Why it kills conversions:** Fake-sounding testimonials are worse than no testimonials. They signal that you either invented them or coached your customers to say nothing specific.

**The fix:** Use real quotes with specific details, even if they're less polished.

> "Honestly? I was skeptical. But we went from 2% to 7% conversion rate in six weeks. That's an extra $23K/month. I don't know how it works, but I'm not questioning it." — Sarah Chen, Founder of Relay

Specific numbers. Named person. Slight skepticism that makes it believable. That's a testimonial that converts.

## Mistake #10: The Premature CTA

ChatGPT places calls-to-action before building sufficient case:

> "Our platform helps teams collaborate better. **Start your free trial today!**"

Wait—you've told me almost nothing. Why would I give you my email?

**Why it kills conversions:** CTAs work when readers are ready. Placing them too early—before you've built desire, addressed objections, and established trust—just highlights how little you've said.

**The fix:** Earn the CTA. The general structure:

1. **Hook** — Get attention with a specific problem or outcome
2. **Problem** — Make them feel the pain of the current situation
3. **Solution** — Introduce your approach (not your product yet)
4. **Product** — Now explain what you're offering
5. **Proof** — Show evidence it works
6. **Objections** — Address why they might hesitate
7. **CTA** — Now ask for action

The CTA belongs at step 7, not step 2. Earlier CTAs can work as "escape valves" for ready buyers, but the main CTA needs to follow the full argument.

---

## Mistake #11: No Personality, No Voice

ChatGPT writes in a voice that belongs to no one:

> "We are committed to delivering excellence in every interaction. Our team is passionate about helping clients achieve their goals through innovative solutions and dedicated support."

This could be any company. It has no personality, no point of view, no humanity.

**Why it kills conversions:** People buy from people, not corporations. Faceless copy creates no connection. If your copy sounds like it could come from any competitor, you've given readers no reason to choose you specifically.

**The fix:** Let personality show through opinions, stories, and informal language.

> "Look—most marketing software is built by engineers who've never run a campaign. We built this because we were tired of tools that looked good in demos and failed in real life. If you've ever rage-quit a dashboard, you're our people."

That has a voice. It has an opinion. It sounds like a human wrote it because a human did.

## The Pre-Publish Checklist

Before any ChatGPT-assisted copy goes live, check for all 11 mistakes:

- [ ] **Opening:** Does it start with something specific, or does it throat-clear?
- [ ] **Benefits:** Is every benefit backed by evidence?
- [ ] **Positioning:** Does it speak to a specific reader, not everyone?
- [ ] **Voice:** Is it active voice, not passive?
- [ ] **Jargon:** Could a smart outsider understand every sentence?
- [ ] **So what:** Does every feature connect to a reader outcome?
- [ ] **Urgency:** Is any urgency real and explained?
- [ ] **Formatting:** Is it scannable with short paragraphs and headers?
- [ ] **Testimonials:** Are quotes specific and believable?
- [ ] **CTA timing:** Has the copy earned the ask?
- [ ] **Personality:** Does it sound like a human with opinions?

If you can't check all boxes, you're publishing copy that will underperform. The AI saved you time—spend some of that time on fixes.

## The Bottom Line

ChatGPT is a powerful starting point, but its default outputs share predictable weaknesses that hurt conversion rates.

The good news: these mistakes are consistent. Once you know the patterns, you can spot them in seconds and fix them in minutes.

The better news: most of your competitors won't bother. They'll publish ChatGPT output with light edits and wonder why it's not working. That's your competitive advantage.

AI writes fast. You write persuasively. Together, that's how you win.
