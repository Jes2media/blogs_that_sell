You've read the warnings about AI copy. You know it sounds generic. You've seen the lists of mistakes to avoid.

But you're still staring at 800 words of ChatGPT output wondering: **What exactly do I do now?**

This is the step-by-step editing process. Not theory—a concrete workflow you can apply to any AI-generated copy to transform it from generic to persuasive.

Let's edit a real example together.

---

## The Starting Point: Raw ChatGPT Output

Here's actual ChatGPT output for a landing page selling email marketing software to small businesses:

> **Transform Your Email Marketing Today**
>
> In today's competitive digital landscape, effective email marketing is more important than ever. Whether you're a small business owner, entrepreneur, or marketing professional, the ability to connect with your audience through compelling email campaigns can make all the difference in achieving your business goals.
>
> Our comprehensive email marketing platform offers everything you need to:
> - Create beautiful, professional emails
> - Grow your subscriber list effortlessly
> - Automate your marketing campaigns
> - Track and analyze your results
> - Improve engagement and conversions
>
> With our intuitive drag-and-drop editor, powerful automation features, and detailed analytics, you'll have all the tools you need to take your email marketing to the next level.
>
> Thousands of businesses trust our platform to deliver results. Join them today and see the difference for yourself!
>
> **Start Your Free Trial Now**

This is... fine. It's not wrong. But it won't convert well because it could describe literally any email marketing tool. There's no specificity, no voice, no compelling reason to choose this over the 47 other options.

Let's fix it.

---

## Step 1: Delete the Throat-Clearing (30 seconds)

The first paragraph is pure warm-up. It says nothing specific and earns no attention.

**Delete:** "In today's competitive digital landscape, effective email marketing is more important than ever. Whether you're a small business owner, entrepreneur, or marketing professional, the ability to connect with your audience through compelling email campaigns can make all the difference in achieving your business goals."

**What's left:** We now start with the bullet list. But that's not a strong opening either. We'll fix that in a moment.

**Rule:** If your first paragraph could introduce any competitor's product, cut it.

---

## Step 2: Find the Real Opening (2 minutes)

The current structure buries the lead. We need to start with something the reader actually cares about.

**Ask yourself:**
- What problem does this solve?
- What's the reader frustrated by right now?
- What outcome are they hoping for?

For email marketing software aimed at small businesses, the real problems are:
- Their emails aren't getting opened
- They don't have time to write campaigns
- They're paying for a tool they can't figure out
- They know email matters but keep putting it off

**New opening:**

> Your emails are going to spam. Or they're opened but ignored. Or you're so overwhelmed by your current tool that you haven't sent a campaign in three months.
>
> You know email marketing works—you just need software that doesn't require a PhD to operate.

Now we've earned attention. The reader either recognizes themselves or doesn't—but they're no longer skimming past generic statements.

---

## Step 3: Rewrite the Benefit List With Evidence (5 minutes)

The original bullets are generic claims without evidence:

> - Create beautiful, professional emails
> - Grow your subscriber list effortlessly
> - Automate your marketing campaigns
> - Track and analyze your results
> - Improve engagement and conversions

Every email platform claims these. They mean nothing.

**For each bullet, ask:** "Says who? Prove it."

| Original | With Evidence |
|----------|---------------|
| "Create beautiful, professional emails" | "Build emails in under 10 minutes using 50+ templates designed by conversion specialists" |
| "Grow your subscriber list effortlessly" | "Built-in signup forms that add 23% more subscribers than generic popups (based on user data)" |
| "Automate your marketing campaigns" | "Set up a complete welcome sequence in 15 minutes—once—then never touch it again" |
| "Track and analyze your results" | "See exactly which emails drive sales, not just opens, with revenue attribution" |
| "Improve engagement and conversions" | "Users average 34% higher click rates after switching from Mailchimp" |

These might not be your real numbers—you'd fill in actual data. The point is: **every benefit needs backup or it's just noise.**

---

## Step 4: Pick a Specific Reader (2 minutes)

The original tries to appeal to everyone:

> "Whether you're a small business owner, entrepreneur, or marketing professional..."

This is the "something for everyone" trap. When you speak to everyone, you resonate with no one.

**Pick your best customer.** For this example, let's say it's:

*Service-based business owners doing $200K-$2M who know they should be emailing their list but aren't, because their current tool is too complicated.*

**New positioning:**

> Built for service businesses that have outgrown Mailchimp but don't need enterprise complexity. If you're spending more time fighting your email tool than writing emails, you're in the right place.

Now the right people feel seen—and the wrong people self-select out.

---

## Step 5: Add the "So What?" Connector (3 minutes)

Features need to connect to outcomes. The original mentions features without explaining why they matter:

> "With our intuitive drag-and-drop editor, powerful automation features, and detailed analytics..."

For every feature, complete the sentence: **"...which means you can..."**

**Rewrite:**

> The drag-and-drop editor means you can build professional emails without touching code—or waiting for your designer to be available.
>
> Automation means you can nurture leads while you sleep. Set up a 5-email welcome sequence once, and it runs forever.
>
> Revenue tracking means you finally know which emails actually drive sales—not just which ones get opened and ignored.

Same features. Now they matter to the reader's life.

---

## Step 6: Replace the Generic Social Proof (2 minutes)

The original:

> "Thousands of businesses trust our platform to deliver results."

This is the weakest possible social proof. "Thousands" is vague. "Trust" is a claim. "Results" is undefined.

**Replace with specific proof:**

> "2,847 service businesses switched to us last year. Here's why:"
>
> "I was paying $99/month for Mailchimp and sending maybe one email a quarter because I couldn't figure out the damn thing. Switched to [Product] in January, sent 12 campaigns by March, and added $14K in revenue from a single reactivation sequence." — Maria Chen, Bookkeeping firm owner

One specific story beats a thousand vague claims.

---

## Step 7: Fix the CTA (1 minute)

The original:

> "Start Your Free Trial Now"

Generic. Doesn't tell you what you're getting or what happens next.

**Better:**

> "Start your 14-day free trial—no credit card required. You'll have your first campaign built before lunch."

Now you know the terms (14 days, no card) and you have a concrete vision of what happens (first campaign today).

## The Editing Checklist

Use this for every piece of AI copy:

### Pass 1: Cut the Filler (1-2 minutes)
- [ ] Delete generic opening paragraphs
- [ ] Remove "in today's world" and "more than ever" statements
- [ ] Cut any sentence that could describe a competitor

### Pass 2: Add Specificity (5-7 minutes)
- [ ] Replace vague benefits with evidence or numbers
- [ ] Pick one specific reader—let others self-select
- [ ] Connect features to outcomes ("which means you can...")

### Pass 3: Build Trust (3-5 minutes)
- [ ] Replace generic social proof with specific stories
- [ ] Add real numbers, even if smaller than "thousands"
- [ ] Include details that couldn't be fabricated

### Pass 4: Add Voice (2-3 minutes)
- [ ] Read aloud—does it sound like a human?
- [ ] Add opinions, personality, informal phrases
- [ ] Remove corporate-speak and jargon

### Pass 5: Fix the CTA (1 minute)
- [ ] Make terms clear (length, cost, commitment)
- [ ] Add concrete next step or outcome
- [ ] Consider objection-handling near the CTA

**Total time: 15-20 minutes**

That's the real time savings from AI. Not zero editing—but 15 minutes of editing instead of 2 hours of writing from scratch.

## Common Editing Scenarios

### Scenario: The copy is structurally fine but boring

**Fix:** Add voice and personality. Insert opinions. Use "you" and "your" more. Read it aloud and rewrite anything that sounds robotic.

### Scenario: The copy makes claims but nothing feels believable

**Fix:** Add evidence to every benefit. If you don't have data, use specific examples, case studies, or testimonials. Cut claims you can't support.

### Scenario: The copy is too long and unfocused

**Fix:** Pick your single most important message. Cut everything that doesn't support it. Often the best version is half as long as the AI output.

### Scenario: The copy is technically accurate but doesn't persuade

**Fix:** You probably have features without outcomes. For every feature, ask "So what?" and add the answer. Connect everything to the reader's life.

### Scenario: The copy sounds like everyone else

**Fix:** Add what makes you different. What's your opinion? What do you do that competitors don't? What would you never do? Differentiation lives in specificity.

## The 80/20 of AI Copy Editing

If you're short on time, these three edits have the highest impact:

1. **Rewrite the first paragraph** to be specific to your reader's problem (not generic industry statements)

2. **Add evidence to your top 3 benefits** (numbers, timeframes, testimonials)

3. **Replace generic social proof** with one specific customer story

Do just these three things and your copy will outperform 90% of AI-generated content that goes straight from ChatGPT to publish.

---

## The Bottom Line

AI copywriting isn't "write copy for me." It's "give me a starting point I can transform."

The transformation takes 15-20 minutes—but it's where all the conversion power lives. Generic AI output converts poorly. Edited AI output that includes specificity, evidence, voice, and real social proof converts well.

The workflow:
1. Generate with AI (2 minutes)
2. Edit with this process (15-20 minutes)
3. Publish copy that actually works

That's still faster than writing from scratch. You just can't skip step 2.
