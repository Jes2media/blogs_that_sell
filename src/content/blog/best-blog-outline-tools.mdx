A good outline is half the writing done.

When you know exactly what you're going to say and in what order, the actual writing flows faster. You don't get stuck wondering what comes next. You don't realize halfway through that your structure doesn't work.

Outline tools can help—but only if you use the right ones for how you think. Here's a practical review of the best options, from AI-powered generators to simple templates.

## What Makes a Good Blog Outline Tool

Before comparing tools, let's define what we're looking for:

**Speed:** The tool should make outlining faster, not slower.

**Flexibility:** It should adapt to your thinking style, not force you into a rigid format.

**Integration:** It should fit into your existing workflow without friction.

**Quality output:** The outlines it produces (or helps you produce) should actually be useful.

Different tools excel at different things. The best choice depends on how you work.

## AI-Powered Outline Generators

These tools use AI to generate outline suggestions based on your topic or keywords.

### ChatGPT / Claude

**Best for:** Custom outline generation with your specific requirements

The general-purpose AI assistants are surprisingly effective for outline generation. Give them your topic, audience, and goals, and they'll suggest structures.

**Strengths:**
- Highly customizable to your needs
- Can incorporate your specific requirements
- Good at suggesting angles you hadn't considered
- Free or inexpensive

**Weaknesses:**
- Requires good prompting to get useful output
- Can suggest generic structures without guidance
- No specialized blog features

**Best prompt approach:**
"Create a blog post outline about [topic] for [audience]. The goal is [conversion goal]. Include an intro hook, [X] main sections with subsections, and a conclusion with CTA. Make it actionable and specific."

**Cost:** Free (ChatGPT, Claude free tiers) or $20/month for premium

### Frase

**Best for:** SEO-focused outline generation

Frase analyzes top-ranking content for your target keyword and suggests outline structures based on what's already working.

**Strengths:**
- Outlines based on competitive analysis
- Shows what topics competitors cover
- Identifies questions people ask
- Integrated content optimization

**Weaknesses:**
- Can lead to derivative outlines
- Monthly cost adds up
- SEO focus may not suit all content types

**Best use case:** When targeting specific keywords and you want to ensure comprehensive coverage of topics competitors address.

**Cost:** Starting at $15/month

### Surfer SEO Content Editor

**Best for:** Data-driven outline structure

Surfer analyzes SERPs and suggests structure, headings, and topics to cover based on what's ranking.

**Strengths:**
- Clear structure recommendations
- Shows exactly what to cover
- Real-time optimization scoring
- Integration with other writing tools

**Weaknesses:**
- Can produce similar content to competitors
- Expensive for occasional use
- Heavy SEO focus may not suit all content

**Best use case:** SEO content teams who need consistent, competitive content structures.

**Cost:** Starting at $69/month

### Jasper (Blog Post Template)

**Best for:** Marketing teams wanting AI-assisted outlines

Jasper's blog post template walks you through outline creation with AI suggestions at each step.

**Strengths:**
- Guided process for beginners
- Marketing-focused suggestions
- Integrates with full writing workflow
- Team features

**Weaknesses:**
- Expensive for individuals
- Templates can feel restrictive
- Output can be generic without customization

**Best use case:** Marketing teams producing volume content who want consistent structure.

**Cost:** Starting at $49/month

## Traditional Outline Tools

These tools help you create and organize outlines manually, without AI generation.

### Workflowy

**Best for:** Infinite nesting and flexibility

Workflowy is a simple outlining tool with unlimited nesting. Everything is a bullet point that can contain more bullet points.

**Strengths:**
- Extremely simple and fast
- Infinite nesting for complex outlines
- Zoom into any section
- Works how your brain works
- Free tier is generous

**Weaknesses:**
- No AI assistance
- Minimal formatting
- May be too simple for some

**Best use case:** Writers who think in hierarchies and want a tool that stays out of the way.

**Cost:** Free (Pro at $5/month)

### Dynalist

**Best for:** Workflowy with more features

Dynalist is similar to Workflowy but with more features—checklists, dates, formatting, and better organization.

**Strengths:**
- More features than Workflowy
- Still fast and clean
- Good free tier
- Export to multiple formats

**Weaknesses:**
- More complex than Workflowy
- No AI features
- Can be overkill for simple outlines

**Best use case:** Writers who want Workflowy-style outlining with more organizational features.

**Cost:** Free (Pro at $10/month)

### Notion

**Best for:** Outlines integrated with your content system

If you already use Notion for content management, its outline features are solid—toggle lists, nested pages, and now AI assistance.

**Strengths:**
- Integrated with broader workflow
- Multiple view options
- Collaboration features
- AI assistance available

**Weaknesses:**
- Slower than dedicated outline tools
- Can be over-featured for just outlining
- Learning curve if you're not already using it

**Best use case:** Writers already in the Notion ecosystem who want everything in one place.

**Cost:** Free (Plus at $10/month, AI at additional $10/month)

### Google Docs Outline View

**Best for:** Simple outlining without new tools

Google Docs has a built-in outline view that auto-generates from your headings. Simple but functional.

**Strengths:**
- Free and familiar
- No new tool to learn
- Outline updates automatically
- Easy collaboration

**Weaknesses:**
- Limited outlining features
- Not designed for outline-first writing
- No AI assistance without add-ons

**Best use case:** Writers who want to outline in the same place they'll write, without learning new tools.

**Cost:** Free

### Scrivener

**Best for:** Long-form content and complex projects

Scrivener is a writing app designed for long-form content with powerful outlining and organization features.

**Strengths:**
- Powerful organization tools
- Corkboard and outline views
- Great for complex projects
- One-time purchase

**Weaknesses:**
- Learning curve
- Overkill for blog posts
- Desktop app only (mostly)

**Best use case:** Writers doing long-form content like books or comprehensive guides.

**Cost:** $49 one-time purchase

## Template-Based Approaches

Sometimes the best "tool" is a simple template you fill in.

### The Standard Blog Outline Template

A simple template that works for most posts:

```
HEADLINE:

HOOK (first paragraph):
- Problem/question this addresses
- Why it matters to reader

MAIN SECTIONS:
1. [Section heading]
   - Key point
   - Supporting point
   - Example

2. [Section heading]
   - Key point
   - Supporting point
   - Example

3. [Section heading]
   - Key point
   - Supporting point
   - Example

CTA SECTION:
- Transition to offer
- Clear call to action

CONCLUSION:
- Summary of key points
- Final thought
- Second CTA
```

**Strengths:** Free, simple, adaptable to any tool.

**Weaknesses:** Requires you to do all the thinking.

### Conversion-Focused Outline Template

For posts designed to drive action:

```
HEADLINE (promise + specificity):

HOOK:
- Agitate the problem
- Promise the solution

BODY:
1. Validate the problem exists
2. Show why common solutions fail
3. Present your approach
4. Prove it works (examples, proof)
5. Address objections

MID-POST CTA:
- Natural transition
- Low-commitment offer

MORE VALUE:
- Deeper dive on one aspect
- Additional proof

FINAL CTA:
- Summarize transformation
- Clear next step
```

## How to Choose

**Choose AI-powered tools when:**
- You want help generating ideas
- You're targeting specific keywords
- You want competitive analysis
- You're okay with subscription costs

**Choose traditional outline tools when:**
- You know what you want to write
- You prefer manual control
- You want free or cheap options
- You think better in hierarchies

**Choose templates when:**
- You want maximum simplicity
- You have a consistent content format
- You don't want new tools
- You're on a tight budget

## My Recommendation

For most bloggers, here's what works:

1. **Start with a template** for your common post types
2. **Use a simple tool** (Workflowy, Dynalist, or Google Docs) to build outlines
3. **Add AI assistance** (ChatGPT/Claude) when you need help with structure or ideas
4. **Only invest in specialized tools** if you're doing heavy SEO content

You don't need expensive tools to create good outlines. You need a clear process and the discipline to use it.

## The Bottom Line

The best outline tool is the one you'll actually use consistently.

Fancy features don't matter if the tool adds friction to your process. Simple tools work fine if they match how you think.

Start simple. Add complexity only when you've outgrown simplicity. And remember: the outline is just the plan. The writing still has to be good.
