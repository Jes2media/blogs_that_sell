You're planning a launch. The question everyone asks: how many emails should I send?

Send too few and you leave money on the table. Over 50% of launch revenue comes in the last 48 hours, which means most of your buyers need multiple touches before they act. But send too many and you burn your list. [70% of consumers have unsubscribed from brands](https://clean.email/blog/insights/email-subscription-fatigue-statistics) in the past three months specifically because of excessive messaging.

The answer isn't a number. It's a framework for finding the point where more emails stop making more money.

## The Math Behind Launch Frequency

Here's what actually happens during a launch sequence.

Email one announces the offer. A small percentage of your list, the most eager buyers, purchases immediately. These are people who were waiting for exactly this offer. They'd buy from a single email.

Emails two through four build the case. You overcome objections, share proof, explain the transformation. Each email converts a new slice of buyers who needed more information or more convincing.

Emails five and beyond create urgency. The deadline approaches. People who were interested but procrastinating finally act. This is where the "50% in the last 48 hours" revenue comes from.

But somewhere in that sequence, the math flips. Each additional email converts fewer new buyers while generating more unsubscribes, more spam complaints, and more long-term disengagement from people who don't buy this time but might buy later.

The question isn't how many emails to send. It's when the incremental revenue from one more email becomes less than the cost of sending it.

## The Diminishing Returns Curve

Research on email engagement shows a consistent pattern: [engagement decays after the second email](https://mailmend.io/blogs/email-fatigue-impact-statistics) in any sequence. The first email gets the highest open rate. The second gets lower. By email five or six, you're reaching a smaller and smaller slice of your audience.

This doesn't mean you should only send two emails. It means you should understand what's happening with each send.

**Emails 1-3:** High engagement, converting your most interested subscribers. These emails are almost always worth sending.

**Emails 4-6:** Moderate engagement, converting the persuadable middle. Worth sending if your offer is strong and your copy gives people new reasons to act.

**Emails 7-10:** Low engagement, but catching deadline-driven buyers. Worth sending only during the final 48 hours when urgency is real.

**Emails 11+:** Diminishing returns territory. Each email costs you more in list fatigue than it generates in revenue. Only justified for very large lists where even small conversion percentages produce meaningful revenue.

The shape of this curve varies by audience and offer, but the pattern holds. Early emails convert interested buyers. Middle emails convert uncertain buyers. Late emails convert procrastinators. After that, you're just annoying people.

## The 7-Email Framework

Based on performance data across DTC and info product launches, here's a sequence structure that captures most available revenue without excessive list damage.

**Pre-launch (2 emails):**

Email 1 lands 5-7 days before cart open. It's a teaser. Something is coming. Build anticipation without revealing everything.

Email 2 lands 1-2 days before cart open. Share the problem you're solving in depth. Prime the audience to want the solution before you offer it.

**Launch window (3 emails):**

Email 3 is launch day. The offer is live. Full details, full pitch. This email does the heavy lifting.

Email 4 lands 24-48 hours after launch. Overcome the top objection. Share a case study or testimonial. Give people who didn't buy immediately a reason to reconsider.

Email 5 lands at the midpoint of your cart-open window. If you're running a 7-day launch, this is day 3 or 4. Share a different angle, a different benefit, a different proof point.

**Close (2 emails):**

Email 6 lands 24 hours before cart closes. This is the deadline warning. Urgency becomes explicit. Summarize the offer. Remind them what they'll miss.

Email 7 lands the morning of cart close, or a few hours before. Final call. This email is short, direct, and focused entirely on the deadline.

Seven emails across 10-14 days captures the full buyer journey: awareness, consideration, decision, urgency. You can adjust timing based on your specific launch window, but the structure holds.

## When to Send More

Some launches justify more than seven emails. The deciding factors are list size, offer complexity, and buyer familiarity.

**Large lists (50,000+):** Even small conversion percentages produce meaningful revenue. An eighth or ninth email that converts 0.1% of 50,000 subscribers is 50 sales. That may justify the list fatigue cost.

**High-ticket offers ($1,000+):** Complex decisions need more information. A single $2,000 sale covers a lot of unsubscribes. Add emails that go deeper on proof, process, and objection handling.

**New audiences:** If your list doesn't know you well, they need more context before buying. Add emails that build credibility and demonstrate expertise.

**Long launch windows (14+ days):** A 3-week launch has room for more emails without overwhelming people. Spread them out. One email every 2-3 days is different from daily emails for a week.

In these scenarios, 10-12 emails can work. But watch your metrics. If unsubscribes spike above 0.5% per email or open rates drop below 15%, you've found your limit.

## When to Send Fewer

Some launches should be shorter and tighter.

**Small, engaged lists:** A 2,000-person list of true fans doesn't need a 10-email sequence. They know you, trust you, and will buy quickly. 4-5 emails is often enough.

**Low-ticket offers (under $100):** A $47 product doesn't need the same persuasion architecture as a $997 course. People make small decisions quickly. 4-5 emails with clear urgency at the end.

**Repeat launches:** If you launch the same product multiple times per year, your list has already heard the pitch. Subsequent launches can be shorter. The first launch might be 7 emails. The third launch might be 4.

**Already-warm audiences:** If you've been nurturing the list with consistent value, they don't need as much selling. You've done the work upfront.

## The Hidden Cost: Post-Launch Damage

Launch fatigue doesn't end when the cart closes. The damage shows up in your metrics for weeks afterward.

[Email list decay averages 22.5% annually](https://mailmend.io/blogs/email-fatigue-impact-statistics), but that rate accelerates after aggressive launches. The subscribers who didn't buy and felt overwhelmed by your emails don't always unsubscribe immediately. Many just stop opening. They stay on your list but become dead weight.

This "silent churn" is harder to measure than unsubscribes but equally damaging. Your open rates drop. Your deliverability suffers. Your next launch performs worse because fewer people see your emails.

The fix is intentional recovery. After a launch, reduce email frequency for 2-3 weeks. Send pure value content with no pitch. Let your list reset. The subscribers who remain engaged are the ones worth keeping.

## The Metrics That Tell You

You don't have to guess when you've hit diminishing returns. The data tells you.

**Unsubscribe rate per email:** Normal is 0.1-0.3%. If a launch email exceeds 0.5%, you've pushed too hard. If multiple emails exceed 0.5%, you're burning the list.

**Open rate trajectory:** Plot your open rates across the sequence. Some decline is normal. A drop of more than 50% from email one to email six suggests fatigue is setting in.

**Revenue per email:** Track how much each email generates. If email seven produces less revenue than email six, and email eight produces less than seven, you've found the inflection point.

**Spam complaints:** Keep this below 0.1%. If launch emails spike complaints, ISPs notice. Your deliverability suffers for months.

Run these metrics for every launch. Over time, you'll develop a clear picture of where your specific audience hits the wall.

## The Decision Framework

Before your next launch, answer these questions:

**How large is your list?** Under 5,000, lean toward fewer emails. Over 25,000, you have room for more.

**How expensive is your offer?** Under $200, keep it tight. Over $500, you can justify more persuasion.

**How familiar is your audience?** New subscribers need more. Long-time readers need less.

**How recently did you launch?** If your last launch was 30 days ago, pull back. If it was 6 months ago, you have more room.

**What did the data say last time?** If your previous launch saw unsubscribes spike at email six, that's your ceiling.

Most coaches and course creators land between 5-8 emails for a typical launch. The operators who consistently exceed this without damage have very large lists, very high-ticket offers, or very long gaps between launches.

When in doubt, err on the side of fewer emails. The revenue you leave on the table from email nine is almost always less than the long-term cost of burning your list.
