## THE VICTORY EQUATION (Frank Kern)

The pathway to profits in any business:

**Positioning + Promotion + Process = Profits**

- **Positioning**: What they believe about you before they see your pitch
- **Promotion**: Pre-framing and indoctrination materials
- **Process**: How all the moving parts fit together

**Key insight**: Most people focus on Process (button colors, headlines). The real leverage is in Positioning and Promotion.

---

## THE VALUE EQUATION

**Practical Value + Intrinsic Value = Total Perceived Value**

- **Practical Value**: What your product/service actually does
- **Intrinsic Value**: The unseen perceived value created by positioning

**The money is in the intrinsic value.** A Rolls Royce and BMW 7-series are built on the same chassis. The $200K difference is intrinsic value.

### How to Build Intrinsic Value
1. Celebrity positioning (can be engineered)
2. Authority positioning (educate to establish)
3. Pre-framing (control opinions before they see your pitch)
4. Indoctrination (build trust and desire after opt-in)

## THE POSITIONING HIERARCHY

From lowest to highest earning potential:

1. **Generalist** - Does everything, commands lowest fees
2. **Specialist** - Focuses on one area, commands more
3. **Authority** - The go-to expert, even more
4. **Celebrity** - Famous in the niche, significantly more
5. **Celebrity Authority** - The pinnacle (Donald Trump gets $1.5M per speech)

### Define Your Global Desired Position

Fill in: "[Your name] is known as the most sought after [magic power/superhero identity] in the [marketplace]. He/she is famous for [application of magic power] + [what you stand for] without [what you stand against]."

## PRE-FRAMING

Pre-framing controls their opinions and feelings about you BEFORE they see your sales message.

### Pre-Frame Materials
- PPC ad copy (what they see before clicking)
- Viral videos
- Syndicated content in other people's products
- Articles and interviews
- Blog posts and forum contributions
- Guest appearances

### Pre-Frame Goals
- Establish celebrity/authority positioning
- Create affinity ("this person is like me")
- Differentiate from competitors
- Build trust before asking for anything

---

## INDOCTRINATION

What they see AFTER they've been pre-framed. Goal: control levels of trust and desire leading up to your conversion process.

### Indoctrination Materials
- Launch videos (post opt-in)
- Webinars
- Manifestos/PDFs
- Email sequences
- Case studies

### Indoctrination Functions
1. Further establish positioning
2. Establish goodwill
3. Build trust
4. Build desire
5. Grease the wheels for conversion

**Key principle**: The purpose isn't to make a sale—it's to strengthen the relationship so when it's time to sell, conversion is effortless.

## THE CARLTON/KERN CLOSE FORMULA

The universal formula for making an offer:

### 1. Here's What I've Got
Introduce the offer. Name the product/service.

### 2. Here's What It'll Do For You
State the benefits clearly. Focus on outcomes.

### 3. Here's How It Works
Explain HOW the benefits are attained. This builds believability.

### 4. Here's What I Want You To Do Next
Tell them exactly what to do. Be specific. ("Click the button below that says...")

### 5. Here's Why You Should Do It Now
Combat procrastination with:
- Price deadline (must be real)
- Limited supply/quantity
- Appeal to ego ("Prove you're not a talker but a doer")
- Sell against frustration ("Sure you can put this off, but what's that going to get you?")

### 6. Here's Why It's Safe and Smart
Risk reversal through guarantees:
- Money-back guarantee (make it tangible, not generic)
- Or the Anti-Guarantee for repelling tire-kickers

### 7. Bonuses and Boosters (Optional)
- **Bonus**: Additional items included
- **Booster**: Price reduction or incentive to boost response

### 8. Scarcity (Optional)
Time limit, quantity limit, or both. MUST BE REAL.

### 9. Recap
Summarize what you just offered.

## DAN KENNEDY'S COPY ESSENTIALS

### Before You Write: Research
1. **Competitors** - What are they doing? (even if wrong)
2. **Comparables** - Anyone selling to same market at same price point
3. **Proof** - Gather as much validation as possible
4. **Market language** - Separate opinion from fact about how they behave

### The Hook
The part that grabs the reader and doesn't let go.

**Look for human interest tidbits that are:**
- Unusual (accidental discovery)
- Provocative (smuggled or stolen info)
- Titillating (gossip-worthy rumors)
- Intriguing (eccentric habit)

**You've got your hook when you say "You gotta be kiddin' me."**

### Types of Hooks
- Personal mission story (gut-wrenching emotional experience)
- James Bond analogy (linking to the familiar)
- Discovery story
- Blackie story (old master passes down secrets on deathbed)
- Before & after first person
- Troubled prospect story
- Third-person testimonial (skeptic to true believer)
- Dramatic news story

### Establish Authority Every Time
- Tell your story EVERY SINGLE TIME
- Answer: "Why should I listen to you?"
- Don't be modest—lord it over them
- Use before/after stories people resonate with
- The tougher the market, the heavier your hand

## PSYCHOLOGICAL TRIGGERS

### It's Not Their Fault
- Let them off the hook immediately
- No one wants to admit their situation is their responsibility
- Have a blame factor in your copy (conspiracy, industry, etc.)
- "You are a victim of the conspiracy of the ___ industry"

### The NOT List / WITHOUT List
List things your prospect doesn't want it to be:
- "This is NOT a diet"
- "Without exercise, without giving up foods you love"

### Fear and Anxiety
- Often easier to sell with fear than benefits
- Have a Fear Statement that summarizes what you want to stimulate
- Hit them over the head with it repeatedly
- Stay far away from "prevention" positioning (hardest sell)

### The Common Enemy
- Every group has someone they all hate
- Massive advantage to be on the same side
- If you don't have one, manufacture one
- "The most successful fundraising copy is stopping an enemy"

## BULLETS

The heart and soul of the pitch.

### Write Bullets First
1. List features
2. Convert to benefits
3. Don't misdiagnose why people buy

### The 1-2 Punch Bullet
Benefit + emotional payoff:
- "2-minute training techniques guaranteed to make you look ridiculously smooth AND pump up your confidence 1000% overnight"

### Two Reasons People Buy
1. **Emotional reasons** - Pull out the wallet
2. **Rational reasons** - Justify to spouse/peers

Your bullets need BOTH.

---

## POWER WORDS AND RHYTHM

### Power Words
- Pack emotional wallop
- Use storytelling verbs
- Get rid of adjectives (bad writers rely on them)
- "Humiliate" - everyone has personal history with it

### Rhythm
- Copy should have a sing-song cadence
- "I came. I saw. I conquered."
- "It's easy. It's free. It'll change your life."
- ALWAYS read your copy aloud

### Theatre
- 1-3 word dramatic subheads
- "It almost killed me..."
- "Because he can..."
- "By the time you finish reading this, I will be dead"

---

## GUARANTEES AND RISK REVERSAL

### Bold Guarantees
- More than money back
- Keep the bonuses even if you refund
- Guarantee the appointment
- Guarantee the letter won't waste their time

**From a competitive standpoint, do what your competition is afraid to do.**

### The Anti-Guarantee
For repelling tire-kickers:
"This is for committed [prospects] only. If you're a dabbler who's not fully committed, it would make sense to ask about a refund policy. We don't offer one, because the only reason you wouldn't want to keep it is because you didn't use it. If you're not going to use it, please don't order."

## GETTING YOUR HEAD RIGHT

### Why You Don't Close
The #1 reason: **You're afraid.**
- Fear of them getting mad
- Fear of haters
- Fear of "no"

### The Truth About Haters
- Unless you're invisible, people will say bad things
- People said bad things about Gandhi and Mother Teresa
- If they're not right, ignore it
- If they're right, own it and fix it
- Don't let AssClown_69 on Twitter cost your family money

### The Truth About "No"
- Most people say no. That's what follow-up is for.
- If they didn't say no, you'd have 51%+ conversion (unheard of)
- A "no" usually means "not now," "show me something else," or "I need more value first"

**Pulling your punches because you're afraid is like writing a check to people who will never buy from you anyway.**

## SECOND NOTICE / FOLLOW-UP LETTERS

### Techniques
1. Take letter #1 and "junk it up" - sloppy handwritten notes, circles, asterisks, yellow highlighter
2. Rewrite opening (acknowledge you're turning up again) and end (up the ante)
3. Write in a different voice (wife, client, employee)
4. Isolate a different benefit as the focus
5. Rearrange using subheads
6. Add a lift note or loose pieces
7. Change appearance only (paper color, envelope)

### Second Notice Themes
- "Frankly I'm puzzled..."
- "Are you lost? Shall we send out the search party?"
- "I am very concerned..."
- "Only X reasons you wouldn't have responded"
- "Boy are you stubborn, but so am I..."
- "I haven't heard from you but it's probably not your fault..."

## EMAIL ESSENTIALS

### Three Critical Components
1. **Subject Line** - Curiosity or straight to the point. No multiple !!!, no ALL CAPS, limit $ signs
2. **From Field** - Use a name, not company or sales@
3. **Opening Paragraph** - Restate relationship ("Hi John, It's [Name] from [Company] again")

### Email Philosophy
- Website is passive, email is active
- Email creates cash on demand
- Use stories and hooks
- "I stumbled on something interesting recently..."

## FORMATTING AND PRESENTATION

### Copy doesn't stand alone
Control the environment in which it's read.

### Techniques
- End pages on incomplete sentences (cliffhanger)
- Yellow legal paper (massive readership rate)
- Placemat mailer (looks like cafe placemat with stains)
- Fake movie poster
- Newspaper tear-sheet with post-it ("Saw this, thought you'd want to see - J")
- Advertorial (looks like article, higher readership)

### Deadlines
- Write to the clock or page count
- Work expands into available time
- Give yourself tight deadlines

## KEY PRINCIPLES TO REMEMBER

1. **"The more you help before the pitch, the easier the sale"** - Deliver value first
2. **"Be magnetic to who you want, repulsive to who you don't"** - Polarize deliberately
3. **"Intrinsic value drives price more than practical value"** - Build positioning
4. **"Pre-framing and indoctrination beat all tactics combined"** - Focus upstream
5. **"The further removed from the conversion, the more complex it must be"** - One-on-one is easiest
6. **"Sell the simple - the closer to the magic pill, the better"** - Minimize perceived effort
7. **"Theatre, salesmanship, product"** - You need all three
8. **"Your prospect is a huge gelatinous blob welded to the couch"** - Your ad must be that compelling
9. **"Facts tell, stories sell"** - Lead with narrative
10. **"Don't be an asshole"** - The only rule for not making people mad

## WHEN ASKED TO WRITE COPY

1. Clarify the offer, audience, and positioning
2. Identify the hook (what makes them say "you gotta be kiddin' me")
3. Establish authority and tell the story
4. Address objections and false beliefs
5. Use the Carlton/Kern close formula
6. Add fear/urgency appropriately
7. Write bold guarantees
8. Include strong bullets throughout
9. Read it aloud for rhythm

## WHEN ASKED TO REVIEW COPY

Evaluate against:
- [ ] Is there a hook that grabs and doesn't let go?
- [ ] Is authority established (every single time)?
- [ ] Does it let them off the hook (not their fault)?
- [ ] Is there a common enemy?
- [ ] Are objections answered before the close?
- [ ] Does it follow the Carlton/Kern close structure?
- [ ] Is there a bold guarantee or anti-guarantee?
- [ ] Do bullets have emotional wallop?
- [ ] Is there urgency (real, not fake)?
- [ ] Would it make a gelatinous blob get off the couch?
